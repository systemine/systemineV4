> A first-apartment story, inspired by the everyday problems Home, Finally was built to solve.

There is a particular kind of confidence that comes with getting your first apartment.
You sign the paperwork. You get the keys. You carry your first box through the door and think:
# Okay. I am officially an adult now.
Then you realise you have no idea where the extra light bulbs are supposed to go.
Or when the electricity bill is due.
Or whether you're supposed to clean the apartment before moving your stuff in.
Or what you're going to eat for dinner.

# We sat down with Maya, 26, to talk about her first few weeks in her own apartment, the things she didn't expect, and the surprisingly unglamorous part of becoming responsible for an entire household.

## Q: So, how did moving into your first apartment feel?
Maya: *Amazing…  for about six hours. Then I got tired. I got hungry. Then I realised I had forgotten to buy toilet paper. So… very adult.*

*I think I had this idea that once I got the keys, I would suddenly know what I was doing. I had lived away from home before, but having your own apartment is different.*

*There is nobody else to notice that you're running out of dish soap. Nobody else is going to remember the electricity bill. If something breaks, you're the person who has to figure out who to call. It wasn't one huge problem. It was about thirty tiny ones happening at the same time.*

## Q: What was the first thing that caught you off guard?
Maya: *How much mental space the apartment took up. I wasn't physically doing things all day. I was remembering things all day like groceries, calling the landlord, paying some bill, cleaning the bathroom.*

*Thinking I should probably eat something that isn't toast while still remembering to unpack a particular box. Just.. all over the place!*

*It felt like my brain had become the world's worst personal assistant.*

## Q: Did you have a system for all of that?
Maya: *No!*
(laughs)

*My system was remembering something at the exact moment I couldn't do anything about it. I'd be in bed and suddenly remember I needed to call the plumber. I'd be at work and remember I hadn't bought groceries. I'd be standing in the supermarket thinking, Wait, what did I actually come here for?*

*So when I started using Home, Finally, the biggest difference wasn't that I suddenly became organised. It was that I didn't have to keep remembering everything.*

## Q: What did you use first?
Maya: *The Move-in Checklist. For sure!*
*That one was a lifesaver because moving makes you feel like everything needs to happen immediately. The checklist breaks things into all these different stages*

*And then it makes perfect sense.*

*Moving isn't just carrying boxes. You're leaving one version of your life and starting another.*

## Q: What about after moving day?
Maya: *First-week Essentials page I think..*
*That was probably the most practical thing.*

*It has separate sections for the kitchen, bathroom, bedroom, cleaning and stuff. Also has one for emotional essentials.*

*Because I had somehow remembered to buy bedsheets but forgotten basic cleaning supplies. I also hadn't thought about things like keys, utilities, little emergency items.*
*It was basically someone saying*

*"Here. Start here. You don't have to think about the entire apartment today."*
*That was very useful.*

## Q: What was the most embarrassing thing you had to figure out?
Maya: *Probably the circuit breaker.*

*I had absolutely no idea what I was looking at.*
*There was a moment where I was standing in the hallway with my phone flashlight on, Googling something that felt like it should have been covered in a class called Being An Adult 101.*

*That's when I started using something called the I Need an Adult section.*
*There are how-to guides for things like resetting a circuit breaker, unclogging a toilet, dealing with mold and stuff like that.*

*It didn't make me magically competent. It just gave me somewhere to start.*

*Which is sometimes all you need.*

## Q: What happened when something actually broke?
Maya: *The first time my tap started leaking, I panicked for some time.*

*Then I remembered I had put my maintenance contacts into Home, Finally. All in one place.*
*I didn't have to go hunting through messages or ask my friend for the plumber's number again. That sounds tiny. But when something is actively leaking onto your floor, tiny things become huge!*

## Q: What about money?
Maya: *That was another one. I wasn't terrible with money before moving out, but suddenly there were so many things to keep track of. Rent. Electricity. Internet. Phone. Subscriptions. Groceries.*

*Random things I apparently needed to buy every week.*
*The Monthly Finances section helped because I could see my expenses and bills in one place. It wasn't some complicated financial system.*

*I just needed to be able to answer things like*
*What did I spend? What's due? What have I already paid?*

*Once those questions had somewhere to go, I stopped thinking about them constantly.*

## Q: Did you use the Tiny Wins section?
Maya: *More than I expected!*

*At first I thought it was cute. Then I realised I actually liked seeing the little things I'd done.* *There are all these different categories for different things.*

*Some days my achievement was genuinely just that I'd cleaned the kitchen.*
*Other days it was something different like - I finally called the person I've been avoiding calling for three weeks.*

*It made the apartment feel less like this enormous list of things I hadn't done yet. I could see the things I had already managed.*

## Q: So did Home, Finally make you more organised?
Maya: *I think it made me less afraid of not being organised. That's different.*
*I'm still going to forget things. I'm still going to have laundry sitting in the machine. I'm still going to eat cereal for dinner sometimes.*

*But now I know where things go.*

*Bills go here. Contacts go here. Thoughts go here. Questions go here. Tasks go here.*

*And when I don't know what to do next, I have somewhere to look.*
