Then, approximately seventeen minutes later:

*When is rent due?*

*Who do I call if the sink starts leaking?*

*Do I need to buy dish soap?*

*Why is there no food in my house?*

*What exactly am I supposed to do about this weird smell?*

*Am I supposed to know how to reset a circuit breaker?*

Welcome to adulting.

Your new apartment doesn’t really come with an instruction manual. You are handed the keys and expected to somehow know how to operate an entire household.

So we made one.

Home, Finally is a Notion system designed around the actual problems that show up when you’re living on your own. Not the glamorous ones. The little things that quietly pile up until your brain feels like it has 47 browser tabs open.

Here are twelve of them.

## 1. Problem: You have no idea what you’re supposed to do first.

Moving day has a funny way of turning every task into an emergency.

There are boxes everywhere. You need to check the apartment. You need to figure out utilities. You need to make sure your doors actually lock. You need to buy approximately 900 things.

And you’re standing there thinking:

*Where do I even begin?*

> Home, Finally gives you the Move-in Checklist.
It’s broken into five phases:

Before You Move In → The First Hour → Protect Yourself → Make It Livable → Emotional Move-In

So instead of holding the entire move in your head at once, you have a sequence to follow.

One thing at a time.

Much nicer.

## 2. Problem: You moved in and suddenly realised you own nothing useful.

You have furniture.

You have clothes.

You have your laptop.

Somehow you don’t have toilet paper.

Or cleaning supplies.

Or a functioning kitchen.

Or anything remotely resembling the ingredients required to feed yourself.

> Home, Finally gives you First-week Essentials.
The page breaks your immediate needs into:

- Kitchen
- Bathroom
- Bedroom
- Cleaning & Maintenance
- Adulting Infrastructure
- Emotional Essentials

It answers the wonderfully unglamorous question:

*“What do I actually need to survive the next seven days?”*

Not everything you might eventually want.

Just the things that make the first week considerably less chaotic.

## 3. Problem: Something breaks and you don’t know who to call.

The tap is leaking.

The electricity has gone weird.

You locked yourself out.

The landlord isn’t answering.

And suddenly you are attempting to remember the name of a plumber your neighbour mentioned three months ago.

> Home, Finally gives you a Maintenance & Contacts page.
You can keep all your important numbers in one place, including:

# Landlord. Building security. Electrician. Plumber. Building maintenance. Locksmith. Nearest hospital. Nearest pharmacy. Trusted cab. Neighbour or trusted person.

There is also space for notes about what each contact is for.

So Future You doesn’t have to conduct an archaeological excavation through old WhatsApp messages during an actual plumbing emergency.

## 4. Problem: You don’t know how to do basic adult things.

Nobody teaches you this stuff.

At some point you’re just expected to know.

How do you unclog a toilet?

How do you reset a circuit breaker?

How do you do laundry without destroying your clothes?

How do you deal with mold?

How do you talk to building maintenance?

> Home, Finally has an entire page called I Need an Adult (How-To’s).
It collects practical questions you may suddenly find yourself asking, including:

*How do I pay bills?*

*How do I cook when I have zero energy?*

*How do I know if I need a doctor?*

*How do I handle a panic attack alone?*

And, naturally:

*Why is my sink making demon noises?*

Because apparently that is also part of the curriculum.

## 5. Problem: You keep forgetting when bills are due.

You remembered rent.

You forgot electricity.

You remembered electricity.

You forgot the internet.

You remembered the internet.

You forgot that you have three subscriptions quietly nibbling at your bank account.

Home, Finally gives your money somewhere to go.
The Monthly Finances page includes a daily expense tracker and a dedicated bill tracker.

You can keep track of:

Rent
Electricity
Internet
Water
Phone
Subscriptions

There are fields for amounts, due dates, autopay, payment status, priority and past-due status.

There is also a little section called Financial Oopsie Prevention, because sometimes financial management is less “build a diversified portfolio” and more:

Did you actually check your bank balance?

## 6. Problem: You keep spending money without really knowing where it’s going.

It’s surprisingly easy to spend $5 here, $12 there, order food twice, buy something because it was on sale and then look at your account wondering where the month went.

> The expense tracker helps you see it.
You can log daily spending and view it through a calendar, alongside your monthly totals.

No elaborate budgeting philosophy required.

Just:

What did I spend?

When did I spend it?

What is my month actually looking like?

Sometimes seeing the numbers is enough to stop the financial fog.

## 7. Problem: You are hungry, exhausted and absolutely unwilling to cook.

This is not a character flaw.

Sometimes you have 3% battery left and the thought of chopping an onion feels like an Olympic event.

> Home, Finally has Recipes to Keep You Fed.
And these are not trying to turn you into a lifestyle influencer.

You’ve got things like:

# Depression Pasta™

# Instant Noodles, But With Self-Respect

# Executive Dysfunction Potatoes™

# The “I Have 23.99 Until Payday” Omelette

# The “My Fridge Has Vibes, Not Food”

and

# The “I Accidentally Meal Prepped” Soup

There is also a water intake tracker and an Emergency Nutrition Cheatsheet covering carbs, protein, vegetables, calcium, vitamin C and potassium.

The goal is pretty simple:

Eat something. Drink some water. Continue existing.

## 8. Problem: Your basic needs somehow became optional.

You showered yesterday.

Probably.

You haven’t been outside in three days.

Your water bottle has been sitting beside you untouched.

You forgot your medication.

You have been surviving entirely on caffeine and vibes.

Home, Finally has a Needs Maintenance Tracker.
You can track basic things such as:

Water
Protein
Showering
Medication
Sleep
Going outside
Moving for five minutes
Speaking to another human
Listening to music
It isn’t asking you to become a perfectly disciplined human.

It’s a reminder that you live in the apartment too.

Your home needs maintenance.

# So do you.

## 9. Problem: You can’t tell whether you’re having an actual emergency or just having A Day™.

Sometimes you suddenly want to cry.

Sometimes you hate everyone.

Sometimes everything feels catastrophically wrong and you have no idea why.

> The Things Nobody Tells You page has a place for that.
It includes a section called Things That Feel Like Emergencies BUT Aren’t, with questions such as:

Why do I wanna cry?

Why do I suddenly hate everyone?

And before you officially declare adulthood a scam, there is a tiny checklist:

☐ You are not hungry

☐ You are not dehydrated

☐ You have slept

☐ Mercury is not in retrograde

☐ Your phone battery is above 15%

Scientific? Questionable.

Useful? Surprisingly.

## 10. Problem: Your brain has nowhere to put all the random stuff in it.

You remember something while brushing your teeth.

You have an idea while making coffee.

You suddenly remember you need to call someone.

Then you forget all three.

Or you remember them at 2:43 AM.

> Home, Finally gives you a Brain Dump.
The Journal / Brain Dump page gives you seven spaces to write whatever is currently rattling around upstairs.

And there is no requirement for it to become a goal, habit, reflection, productivity exercise or beautifully organised journal entry.

It can just be a thought.

A rant.

A list.

A paragraph.

A sentence.

A complete mess.

The rule is wonderfully simple:

Nobody is grading your experience of being alive.

## 11. Problem: You feel like you’re failing because your apartment doesn’t look “done.”

You’ve seen the apartments online.

The immaculate kitchens.

The perfectly styled shelves.

The plants that somehow remain alive.

Meanwhile, there is one chair covered in clothes and you’ve been using the same cardboard box as a bedside table for two months.

> Home, Finally has Tiny Wins.
Tiny Wins breaks achievements into:

Home. Adulting. Survival. Emotional. Legendary Achievements.

Because getting your home together isn’t one giant accomplishment.

It’s hundreds of tiny ones.

You assembled something.

You cleaned for fifteen minutes.

You finally bought the thing you’ve been putting off.

You dealt with a bill.

You survived a difficult day.

You made the apartment feel a little more like yours.

Those count!!

## 12. Problem: You thought having a home would make you feel like an adult.

This one is slightly sneaky.

Because sometimes you get everything you’ve wanted.

Your own keys.

Your own space.

Your own furniture.

Your own decisions.

And somehow you still feel like you’re pretending.

Home, Finally doesn’t try to fix that.
It gives you somewhere to put the practical stuff while you figure out the rest.

The checklists handle the move.

The trackers handle the bills.

The contacts handle the emergencies.

The How-To’s handle the questions.

The recipes handle the hungry days.

The needs tracker handles the human being.

The Brain Dump handles the thoughts.

And the Tiny Wins remind you that you’re actually doing more than you think.
