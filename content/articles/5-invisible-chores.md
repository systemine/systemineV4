“Where did I keep the rent agreement?”

“What’s the Wi-Fi password?”

“Which switch controls the water heater?”

“Did I pay the electricity bill this month?”

None of these questions are difficult on their own.

The problem is that they never arrive one at a time.

They pile up quietly until your brain feels like it’s running twenty browser tabs at once.

That’s the part nobody talks about.

## 1. Remembering EVERY bill
Rent is easy because it’s obvious.
- The others?

- Electricity.

- Internet.

- Water.

- Gas.

- Maintenance.

- Subscriptions you forgot you even signed up for.

Every company has a different payment date. Every missed reminder becomes one more thing sitting in the back of your mind.
It’s not the bill that’s stressful.

It’s having to remember that the bill exists.

## 2. Keeping everyone’s phone number somewhere you’ll actually find it

On moving day, everyone hands you a number.

- Your landlord.

- The electrician.

- The plumber.

- The building manager.

- The internet technician.

- The security guard.

You tell yourself you’ll remember who’s who.

Three weeks later your sink starts leaking and you’re scrolling through old WhatsApp chats trying to remember whether “Rick” was the plumber or the guy who delivered your washing machine.

## 3. Knowing where your important documents are

The lease.

Warranty cards.

Purchase receipts.

Your Wi-Fi account details.

The instruction manual for that appliance with seventeen buttons.

You only need these once in a while.

Which is exactly why you never remember where you kept them.

## 4. The tiny maintenance jobs

Replacing a light bulb.

Checking the water purifier.

Buying new batteries.

Restocking medicines.

Replacing the kitchen sponge.

None of these tasks deserve a calendar reminder.

Yet forgetting them makes everyday life slightly more annoying.

## 5. Remembering the tiny things that quietly keep a home running

Where are the spare keys?

When does the lease renew?

What’s the paint colour if a wall gets scratched?

Which cupboard has the extra garbage bags?

Tiny questions.

Tiny answers.

Tiny mental notes.

Your brain keeps collecting them anyway.

## The funny part?

None of this has anything to do with being “bad at adulting.”

> You’re simply carrying dozens of tiny responsibilities that your parents probably handled without you ever noticing.

You didn’t suddenly become forgetful.

Your job description changed overnight.
