# You got the keys. You have the apartment. You may even have a suspicious number of cardboard boxes.
Now what?

There is a strange little gap between “I’m moving into my own place!” and “Wait. Who is supposed to know when the electricity bill is due?”

Nobody really gives you a manual for having a home.

You are expected to somehow know how to keep track of bills, find a plumber, remember when you last changed the bedsheets, feed yourself when you’re exhausted, deal with a mysterious sink noise, buy groceries before you have nothing left to eat, and remain emotionally stable while doing all of this.

Home, Finally was made for that gap.

It is a Notion-based home management system for the practical, mundane, occasionally ridiculous business of running your own life.

Not a productivity cult or a five-step morning routine.

Just one place to put the things your brain shouldn’t have to keep carrying around.

## First: Get Yourself Into the Apartment

The first page you’ll probably want is the Move-in Checklist.

And no, it isn’t just “buy a mattress, unpack boxes, congratulations.”

The checklist is divided into five phases:

- Before You Move In
- The First Hour
- Protect Yourself
- Make It Livable
- Emotional Move-In

![Move-in-Checklist](/images/products/home-finally/move-in-checklist.png)

Because moving house isn’t one event. It’s about a dozen small events wearing a trench coat. Before you arrive, there are things to sort out and check.

During those first few hours, there are things you will want to handle while the apartment is still empty and you’re not standing in the middle of a cardboard-box ecosystem. Then comes the less glamorous stuff: security, keys, locks, utilities, practical setup.

After that, you can start making the place actually livable.

# And finally, there is Emotional Move-In.

Because sometimes you get the keys and instead of feeling like the triumphant protagonist in a coming-of-age movie, you sit on the floor and think:

*Holy shit. I live here now.* 

That counts too.

## Then: Survive the First Week

Once you’ve crossed the threshold, First-week Essentials takes over.

This page answers one deceptively simple question:

> What do I actually need to survive the next seven days?


![First-week-Essentials](/images/products/home-finally/first-week-essentials.png)

Instead of one enormous shopping list containing everything from furniture to decorative candle holders, the essentials are grouped into six areas:

# Kitchen. Bathroom. Bedroom. Cleaning & Maintenance. Adulting Infrastructure. Emotional Essentials.

That last one is deliberate.

You need toilet paper.

You also need things that make the new place feel a little less like a temporary holding facility.

And because the brain occasionally chooses the worst possible moment to stop cooperating, there is an “OPEN ONLY WHEN PANICKING” section too.
No judgment. Just emergency instructions.

## Your Home Has a Control Room Now

Once you’ve survived the move, you arrive at the main Home, Finally dashboard.

![Home-Dashboard](/images/products/home-finally/home.png)

This is where everything starts to connect.

You’ve got quick actions for:

- Expenses
- Contacts
- Grocery lists
- Notes and comments
- Reminders

Then there are your Tiny Wins, grouped into:

- Home
- Adulting
- Survival
- Emotional
- Legendary Achievements

Because apparently keeping yourself alive, remembering to pay a bill and finally assembling that stupid shelf all belong on the same achievement system.

Honestly? Fair.

The dashboard also gives you quick access to the rest of the system, along with dedicated spaces for your finances, maintenance, needs, food, adulting questions and the things nobody tells you about having a home.

There is even an emergency section for the moments when everything goes slightly sideways.

Which brings us neatly to money.

## The Money Stuff: Before Your Bank Account Starts Plotting Against You

Monthly Finances gives your money somewhere to live.

![Monthly-Finances](/images/products/home-finally/monthly-finances.png)

There is a daily expense tracker where you can record what you spend and see it across a calendar.

There is also a dedicated Bills tracker, with space for things like:

- Rent
- Electricity
- Internet
- Water
- Phone
- Subscriptions

You can track the amount, due date, autopay status, whether you’ve paid it, priority and whether something has gone past its due date.

There is also a small but wonderfully necessary section called Financial Oopsie Prevention.
Because sometimes adulthood isn’t about sophisticated financial strategy.

Sometimes it is simply:

☐ Rent paid

☐ Electricity paid

☐ Internet paid

☐ Emergency fund exists

☐ Account balance checked without crying

Progress.

## When Something Breaks, You Already Know Who To Call

Every home eventually produces a problem. A leak. A broken switch. A locked door. A sink making a sound that suggests it has developed opinions.

The Maintenance & Contacts page gives you one place to keep the people who can help.

![Contacts](/images/products/home-finally/contacts.png)

You can store contacts for your:

- Landlord
- Building security
- Electrician
- Plumber
- Building maintenance
- Nearest hospital
- Nearest pharmacy
- Locksmith
- Trusted cab
- Neighbour or trusted person

There is space for phone numbers, whether you’ve saved the number in your phone, and notes about what each contact is useful for.
So when water is suddenly coming from somewhere water should absolutely not be coming from, you don’t have to search through 417 WhatsApp conversations trying to remember which plumber your landlord mentioned six months ago.

The plumber is already there.

Beautiful.

## “I Need an Adult.”

You know the feeling. The toilet is clogged. The circuit breaker tripped. Your clothes need washing and you’re afraid of shrinking something. The sink is making demon noises. You don’t know whether the mold is normal, dangerous, or simply something you should be pretending not to see.

This is why there is an entire I Need an Adult (How-To’s) page.

![How-Tos](/images/products/home-finally/i-need-an-adult.png)

It contains practical guides for questions such as:

# How do I unclog a toilet?

# How do I reset the circuit breaker?

# How do I do laundry without destroying my clothes?

# How do I deal with mold?

# How do I talk to building maintenance?

And because adulthood apparently includes being your own emergency department, there are also guides for:

# How do I pay bills?

# How do I handle a panic attack alone?

# How do I cook when I have zero energy?

# How do I know if I need a doctor?

The goal isn’t to make you magically know everything. It’s to give you somewhere to start when you don’t.

## Feed Yourself, Even When You’re Running on 3%

The Recipes to Keep You Fed section has a very specific philosophy:

You do not need to become a chef because you moved out. You need food.

![Food](/images/products/home-finally/recipies.png)

There are recipes for the particular states of being that seem to occur when you’re living alone:

# Depression Pasta™

# Instant Noodles, But With Self-Respect

# Executive Dysfunction Potatoes™

# The “I Have 23.99 Until Payday” Omelette

# The “My Fridge Has Vibes, Not Food”

And the deeply optimistic:

# The “I Accidentally Meal Prepped” Soup

There is also a water intake tracker and an emergency nutrition cheat sheet covering carbs, protein, vegetables, calcium, vitamin C and potassium.
Because sometimes “taking care of yourself” needs to be reduced to:

*Have you eaten something?*

*Have you had water?*

Excellent. Start there.

## Your Body Is Also Part of the Apartment

This is one of my favourite parts of Home, Finally.

The Things Nobody Tells You page is where the system gets a little more human.

![Things-Nobody-Tells-You](/images/products/home-finally/things-nobody-tells-you.png)

There is a My Needs Maintenance Tracker where you can keep track of basic things such as:

# - Drinking water
# - Eating something with protein
# - Showering
# - Taking medication
# - Sleeping enough
# - Going outside
# - Moving for five minutes
# - Speaking to another human
# - Listening to something you enjoy

It isn’t framed as a perfect daily routine. It is maintenance. Because you are, unfortunately, operating a surprisingly high-maintenance biological machine.

There is also a section for those moments when something feels like an emergency but you’re not quite sure what is happening.

Questions like:

# Why do I suddenly want to cry?

# Why do I suddenly hate everyone?

And before declaring adulthood a failed experiment, there is a quick check:

☐ Are you hungry?

☐ Are you dehydrated?

☐ Have you slept?

☐ Is Mercury in retrograde?

☐ Is your phone battery above 15%?

We cannot promise that Mercury is innocent.

But food and sleep are worth checking first.

## And Then There Are the Things Nobody Warned You About

This section contains little pieces of practical reality that don’t really belong anywhere else.

Things like:

Your apartment will probably never look completely finished.

You will forget groceries.

Something will break at the worst possible financial moment.

Everyone secretly Googles adulting questions.

There will eventually be THE Chair.

Cleaning for fifteen minutes counts as cleaning.

Frozen food is not a moral failure.

And sometimes the achievement of the day is simply remaining indoors and alive.

There is also a small reminder that you are allowed to have an imperfect home.

The point isn’t to maintain some immaculate adult-life fantasy.

The point is to have a place that works for you.

## When Your Brain Needs Somewhere Else to Put Things

And finally, there is Journal / Brain Dump.

Seven blank spaces.

No productivity system breathing down your neck. No requirement to turn your thoughts into goals.

No one is grading your experience of being alive.

![Brain-Dump](/images/products/home-finally/brain-dump.png)

You can write. Complain. Think. Make lists. Leave a completely incoherent paragraph about something that happened at 2:17 AM.

The page itself says it best:

> *This page does not need to be organised, productive, insightful, or beautiful. It only needs to be honest.*

That is the whole point.

## You Don’t Have to Be Good at Adulting

Maybe that’s the thing Home, Finally is really for. Not people who have their lives perfectly sorted. People who are trying to build a life while still figuring out how the machinery works.

You can use the Move-in Checklist when you’re moving. The First-week Essentials when you’re surrounded by boxes. The Finance tracker when the bills start arriving. The Contacts page when something breaks. The How-To’s when you have absolutely no idea what you’re doing. The Recipes when cooking feels impossible. The Needs Tracker when you’ve accidentally forgotten that you are a person with a body. The Brain Dump when your head has 37 tabs open. And the Tiny Wins when you need someone to point out that, actually, you did quite a lot today.

Home, Finally isn’t here to turn you into a perfectly organised adult.

It’s here to make being one a little less lonely, a little less chaotic, and considerably easier to remember. Because your new apartment didn’t come with instructions.
