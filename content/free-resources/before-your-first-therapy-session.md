## Before you begin

Starting therapy can feel strangely difficult even when you know you want help.

You don't need to arrive with a perfectly organized story. You don't need the right words. And you don't need to understand everything that's happening before you walk through the door.

This little space is simply here to help you gather some of what is already there.

Take what feels useful. Leave anything that doesn't.

## Why am I looking for therapy now?

What has been happening lately that made you consider reaching out?

You might think about:
- something that changed
- something that has been going on for a long time
- something that suddenly became harder to manage
- a feeling, pattern, relationship, or situation you want to understand better

Write without trying to make it sound reasonable.

## What would I like to be different?

If therapy were useful to you, what might change?

This doesn't have to be a huge goal.

Maybe you would like to:
- understand yourself better
- feel less overwhelmed
- change a recurring pattern
- communicate differently
- make sense of something that happened
- cope with a difficult situation
- simply have somewhere to talk honestly

What would make you think, "I'm glad I started"?

## What feels difficult to say?

Is there anything you worry you might leave out because it's embarrassing, confusing, painful, or hard to explain?

You don't have to write the whole story here.

Even a few words can be enough to remind you that it's something you may want to bring up.

## What am I worried about?

What are you nervous about when you imagine starting therapy?

There might be concerns about being judged, misunderstood, not knowing what to say, becoming emotional, not making progress, or simply not knowing what therapy will be like.

Write down whatever comes up.

## What would I like my therapist to know?

Is there anything that might help someone understand where you're coming from?

This could include:
- important context about your life
- things you've tried before
- patterns you've noticed
- previous experiences with support
- things that tend to make situations easier or harder

You decide what you want to share and when.

## Questions I might want to ask

You can bring questions to your first session.

Some possibilities:

- How do sessions usually work?
- What happens if I don't know what to talk about?
- How do we decide what to work on?
- How will we know whether this is helping?
- What should I do between sessions?
- Is there anything I should know about confidentiality?
- What happens if I don't feel like we're a good fit?

Add your own questions below.

## One thing I want to remember

Before you begin, write down one thing you don't want to lose sight of.

It could be a hope.

A question.

Something you've been carrying.

Or simply:

**"I don't have to explain everything perfectly."**

---

### A small note

This isn't a test, diagnosis, or substitute for professional care.

It's simply a place to pause, notice what you're carrying, and decide what you might want to bring into the room.

You can change your answers later. You can skip questions. You can bring this with you, or leave it behind.

There is no correct way to begin.
